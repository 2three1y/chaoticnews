A local man tried four different charging cables, reset every setting on his phone and restarted his car twice during a 25-minute commute Monday, only for his phone to connect perfectly the exact second he pulled into his own driveway.

"The map came up," said Doug Ramsdell, 44, still gripping the steering wheel an hour later. "It said, 'You have arrived.' I know, map. I know I have arrived. I have been arriving this whole time."

The connection lasted 11 seconds. Ramsdell then turned off the engine, which CarPlay interpreted as a personal insult.

## A Timeline of Suffering

According to a minute-by-minute reconstruction by Chaotic News Network, the drive went as follows:

1. 7:42 AM: Ramsdell plugs in Cable One. The phone makes the charging sound. The car makes no sound. The car is thinking.
2. 7:46 AM: Cable Two, found in the cupholder, covered in what investigators believe was once a gummy bear.
3. 7:53 AM: Cable Three. The screen displays the word "Connecting" for four full minutes, then switches to the radio, which is playing a mattress commercial.
4. 8:01 AM: Cable Four, borrowed from his son's backpack. His son later confirmed it was "for a different phone. And also a different decade."
5. 8:07 AM: Driveway. Instant connection. Full volume.

Witnesses at a red light on Maple Street say Ramsdell spent the entire light unplugging and replugging the cable "like he was trying to start a lawnmower." A woman in the next lane said she watched him say "please" to the dashboard.

"It wasn't a normal please," said the witness, Brenda Okafore. "It was a hostage please."

## The Phone and the Car Do Not Like Each Other

Experts say Ramsdell's experience is extremely common and has no known cure.

"The phone and the car have to agree on something," explained Dr. Marvin Pluggs, director of the Institute for Wires That Almost Work. "And they have never agreed on anything. The phone thinks the car is old. The car thinks the phone is showing off. They are basically two cousins at Thanksgiving."

Dr. Pluggs said his research team has tested CarPlay in more than 300 vehicles. It connected reliably in only one: a car that was parked inside a showroom, turned off, with no phone in it.

"Our best theory," he said, "is that CarPlay connects when you no longer need it. It is less of a feature and more of a life lesson."

Ramsdell's son, Tyler, 14, said he could have helped but "nobody asked." When asked how he would have fixed it, Tyler said, "Bluetooth." When reminded that Bluetooth had also failed, Tyler shrugged and said, "Then I would've just used my own phone and not talked to him. That's what I always do."

> "It wasn't a normal please. It was a hostage please."

## The Playlist

Ramsdell had spent the entire drive trying to play one specific playlist, titled "DRIVING HYPE 2026." It started at full volume the moment the car was in park, in a closed garage, at 8:07 in the morning.

It was not the playlist. It was a podcast episode titled "Learning to Let Go: Patience in a Busy World." The host's first words, according to Ramsdell, were "Take a deep breath."

His wife, Carla, reportedly ran into the garage thinking there was an intruder. "There was," she said. "It was a man named Gerald talking about breathing."

Ramsdell has since filed a complaint with his car's manufacturer, whose support line put him on hold with a 9-second loop of what he described as "a seatbelt chime, but sadder." A representative eventually answered and introduced himself as Steve. Ramsdell hung up immediately, out of habit.

## Next Steps

Ramsdell says he has a plan for tomorrow's commute: he will start the car, plug in all four cables at once and simply sit in the driveway until something happens.

"If it only works in the driveway," he said, "then I'll live in the driveway."

At press time, Ramsdell had driven around the block three times just to hear one song. The phone disconnected at the corner, reconnected in the driveway and announced, cheerfully, "You have arrived."
