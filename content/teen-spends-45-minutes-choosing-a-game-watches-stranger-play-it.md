An area teen with a free Saturday and a library of 312 games spent 45 minutes scrolling through all of them before deciding the best option was to watch a total stranger play one on YouTube.

“I wanted something chill, but not too chill,” the teen explained. “Something with a story, but not reading. Something new, but that I already know how to play.” No such game exists.

Sources say he opened three games, watched each one’s loading screen, and closed all three “to keep his options open.” At one point he scrolled past the same racing game eleven times.

He eventually settled on a 4-hour video of a man named DragonBreath77 playing a game the teen already owns, has installed, and could have been playing the whole time.

“It’s different when he does it,” the teen said. “He has commentary.”

Gaming psychologist Dr. Lev L. Upp says this is common. “Choosing is a game too,” she explained. “And they are losing it.”

At press time, DragonBreath77 had gone to choose a game himself and was reportedly watching someone else.
