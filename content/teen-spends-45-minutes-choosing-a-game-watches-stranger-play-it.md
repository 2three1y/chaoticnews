An area teen with a completely free Saturday and a library of 312 video games spent 45 minutes scrolling through every single one of them before deciding the best option was to watch a total stranger play one on YouTube.

"I wanted something chill, but not too chill," explained Marcus Delgado-Finch, 16. "Something with a story, but no reading. Something new, but that I already know how to play. Something multiplayer, but where nobody talks to me."

No such game exists. Scientists have been looking since 1985.

## The Scroll

Marcus began his search at 10:02 AM, sitting on the floor in front of his console with a bowl of cereal he would never touch again.

According to a reconstruction by Chaotic News Network, the next 45 minutes went like this:

1. He scrolled past the same racing game 11 times, saying "nah" out loud each time, as if the game could hear him.
2. He opened a fantasy adventure game, watched its 90-second loading screen, read the tip "Remember to save often!" and closed it "to keep his options open."
3. He opened a farming game, created a character, named it "Bread," and quit before Bread could plant anything.
4. He checked the store for new games, even though he already owns 312.
5. He considered "going outside," looked at the window for a moment, and laughed quietly.

His library contains 312 games. He has finished four. One of them was a tutorial.

"I bought most of them on sale," Marcus said. "So technically I saved money. Technically I'm rich."

> "I wanted something chill, but not too chill. Something new, but that I already know how to play."

## DragonBreath77

At 10:47 AM, Marcus put down the controller, picked up his phone and settled on a 4-hour, 12-minute video of a man named DragonBreath77 playing a game Marcus already owns, has installed, and could have been playing the entire time.

"It's different when he does it," Marcus explained. "He has commentary. He has a face cam. He goes 'let's GO' when stuff happens. When I play, nobody goes 'let's GO.' My mom just goes 'is that the thing that's loud?'"

DragonBreath77, whose real name is reportedly Gordon, is 38 and plays games for a living from a room filled with purple LED lights. Reached by email, he confirmed that he also spends 45 minutes choosing a game every morning.

"I have 1,400 games," Gordon said. "Most days I just watch a guy named PixelPrincess play one. She watches me. We are all watching each other. Nobody is playing anything. It's fine."

## A Growing Problem

Gaming psychologist Dr. Lev L. Upp says Marcus's behavior is common and getting more common.

"Choosing is a game too," she explained. "And it is the only game most teens finish. Our research shows that the average teenager spends 31 percent of their free time playing games, 19 percent watching other people play games, and 50 percent scrolling through games, deciding not to play them."

Dr. Upp recommends a simple method called the Two-Minute Rule: pick any game within two minutes, or the console decides for you.

"We tested this with 200 teens," she said. "In every case, the console picked the racing game. Every single time. We don't know why. We are a little scared."

Marcus's younger sister, Nia, 9, says she does not understand the problem. "I have one game," she said. "I play it."

## The Afternoon

By 3 PM, Marcus had finished the entire DragonBreath77 video, watched two "reaction videos" to the same video, and started a 20-minute video titled "Top 10 Games You Should Be Playing Right Now," which he described as "research."

At press time, Marcus had finally picked a game, turned on the console and settled in, at which point his mother called him down for dinner, and he went to bed that night still telling everyone it had been "a really good gaming day."
