[Engadget](https://x.com/engadget) posted on X that your PC's rear USB ports are best for anything that needs full power and speed, while the front ports are convenient but not as reliable. Millions of people looked at the back of their computer and whispered, "No."

## What Actually Happened

The ports on the back of a desktop are usually soldered right onto the motherboard, so power and data take a short trip. Front ports connect through extension cables, and every extra hop loses a little signal.

Front ports also often share one connector on the motherboard, so speed and power get split between them. And makers often use slower USB standards for some of the front ports anyway.

Engadget's example: the ASUS ROG Maximus Z790 Hero motherboard has two 40 Gbps Thunderbolt 4 ports, six 10 Gbps ports and four 5 Gbps ports on the back. The front has one 20 Gbps port and four 5 Gbps ports running off two connectors, so plug two external SSDs in up front and they may share the same 5 Gbps.

Macs do it too. The base Mac mini has front USB-C ports up to 10 Gbps, while the Thunderbolt ports on the back reach 40 Gbps. It's not a universal rule, though: some modern PCs have fast ports up front.

What to plug where: a mouse, keyboard, headphones or a normal flash drive are fine in the front. External SSDs, capture cards and VR headsets belong in the back. Check your computer's specs to see each port's speed.
