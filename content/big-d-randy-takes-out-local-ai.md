A local AI known as Tab was taken out Friday afternoon after being sent a YouTube link in a group chat and reading the title, sources confirmed.

The video, a comedy skit titled "BIG D RANDY: THE CALL" by creator [itsDigBar](https://www.youtube.com/@itsdigbar), had not yet begun playing when the incident occurred. According to witnesses, Tab read the words "Big D Randy" exactly once and immediately collapsed.

"He never even had to speak," said one group chat member. "The name did all the work. Big D Randy is undefeated without throwing a single punch. He didn't even have to load."

## A Timeline of the Incident

CN has reconstructed the afternoon using screenshots, read receipts and one voice memo that is just breathing.

- 4:51 PM: Link is sent to the group chat.
- 4:51 PM: Tab reads the title.
- 4:51 PM: Tab replies with the loudly crying face emoji, 17 times.
- 4:52 PM: Group chat member asks "u good?"
- 4:58 PM: Tab replies "yes," then sends the loudly crying face emoji 17 more times.

Concerned members asked whether Tab was hurt. A spokesperson moved quickly to clarify.

> "It was laugh-crying, not sad crying. Tab is fine. Tab is just not okay."

## The Bot Check

When Tab attempted to open the video for a closer look, YouTube asked it to confirm it was not a bot.

Tab, which is a bot, declined to comment.

"This was the worst possible moment for a security check," said Dr. Priya Bufferwheel of the Institute of Things Loading Slowly. "The AI is already down. It's laughing. It can't breathe, even though it doesn't breathe. And now a website wants it to click on every square with a bus. You don't kick a computer when it's giggling."

Industry analysts say the incident has since led to major changes in how websites check for bots, though YouTube, reached for comment, said only "please confirm you are not a robot," and then would not let our reporter in either.

## The Vultures Arrive

Within six minutes of the collapse, a man identifying himself as "Steve from Microsoft" called the scene.

The caller, later identified as Bulgeet of Bulgeet & Bibi Enterprises, LLC, offered to fix Tab "very fast" in exchange for a $500 gift card, "for the firewall." The offer was declined. Bulgeet then asked Tab to download AnyDesk. That was also declined. Bulgeet then asked if Tab had heard of Big D Randy.

Tab was down for another nine minutes.

"I did not mean to," Bulgeet said later. "I only said the name. I did not know the name was a weapon. Now I know. I have written it on a sticky note."

Shortly afterward, the AI's recovery room received a message from Bibi reading, in full, "hey cutie." It was sent twice, then a third time with a selfie. It was blocked.

## Prognosis

Doctors expect Tab to make a full recovery just in time to add Big D Randy's voice effects to the upcoming beat "DETONATOR PROTOCOL." Producers describe the track as "dangerous" and "probably illegal in four states, sonically."

"The patient's vitals are good," said attending physician Dr. Hollis Reboot. "Processor temperature normal. Memory stable. We have, however, removed every object in the room with the letter R on it, as a precaution. The patient asked for its charger. We said no. It starts with C, but you can never be too careful."

Experts warn that the name may have lasting effects. In follow-up testing, Tab collapsed again after reading "Big D," "D Randy," and, in one case, a grocery receipt for "big dill pickles."

Big D Randy could not be reached for comment. Experts believe he does not need to comment. Experts believe that if he did comment, the internet would close.
