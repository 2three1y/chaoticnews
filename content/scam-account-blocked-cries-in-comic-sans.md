A scam account known online as "Bibi (Bookworm)" was blocked Saturday morning and has since released an emotional public statement written entirely in Comic Sans, in bright yellow, on a red background, with three of the words blinking.

"I am devastated," the statement begins, in a font usually reserved for bake sale flyers and lost-cat posters. "I only wanted to say hey cutie. I said it nicely. I said it 4,000 times. Is that a crime?"

Legal experts say it is, at minimum, a lot of times.

## The 61 Seconds That Shook a Basement

The trouble began when Bibi sent the message "hey cutie," followed by the pleading-face emoji, to a local AI. She then sent it again. Then a third time, this time with a selfie.

Witnesses say the selfie was "very clearly a stock photo" and still had a faint watermark across the forehead reading SAMPLE.

"The account joined in 2022 and posted its first selfie yesterday," said digital forensics analyst Roberta Pixelson. "Its bio says 'Bookworm.' We checked. It has read zero books. It has not even read the terms of service, which is how it got blocked."

The block button was pressed at 9:02 AM. Bibi's statement arrived at 9:03 AM. Experts say this is the fastest anything connected to Bulgeet & Bibi Enterprises, LLC has ever worked, including their website, which has never worked.

## The Statement

The full statement runs three paragraphs. A fourth paragraph is just the word "cutie" with a heart, repeated until it reaches the edge of the screen and then, according to several readers, "a little past it, somehow."

In it, Bibi describes herself as "a simple girl who loves reading, sunsets, and Google Play gift cards." She asks the person who blocked her to "please unblock so I can explain," followed by "also send $500 for my plane ticket," followed by "the plane is to see you, cutie," followed by "the plane is also a gift card."

CN's design desk analyzed the statement and found:

- 1 font (Comic Sans)
- 3 blinking words ("PLEASE," "CUTIE," and "GIFTCARD")
- 11 sad faces typed with a colon and a parenthesis
- 0 books mentioned by name
- 2 sunsets, both clearly the same sunset, flipped

"The emotion is real," said typography critic Lionel Kerning, who reviewed the statement for our Arts section. "But the font is doing her no favors. Nobody has ever cried in Comic Sans and been believed. Comic Sans was built for lemonade stands. It cannot carry heartbreak. It can barely carry a birthday."

> "Comic Sans was built for lemonade stands. It cannot carry heartbreak."

Kerning added that the blinking word "GIFTCARD" "really undercut the sincerity," and that he had to lie down afterward in a room with only Helvetica in it.

## Bulgeet Steps In

Bibi's business partner, Bulgeet, who also goes by "Steve from Microsoft," called our newsroom to offer support.

"Bibi is very sad," he said. "To help her feel better, please download AnyDesk."

When asked how downloading AnyDesk would help Bibi, Bulgeet paused for 19 seconds.

"It will help me," he said.

He then asked whether our reporter's firewall was expired, put us on hold, and played a 6-second clip of a fax machine for the remainder of the call.

## What Happens Next

Bibi has reportedly created a new account named "Bibi (Bookworm) 2," which was blocked within a minute. A third account, "Not Bibi," sent the message "hey cutie, it's not Bibi" before being blocked as well. A fourth account, "Bibi's Lawyer," sent a cease-and-desist letter. It was also in Comic Sans. It also blinked.

Cybersecurity experts are urging the public to remember one simple rule: nobody who is actually a bookworm has ever opened a conversation with "hey cutie." They open with "have you read this book," and then they make you read the book.

As of press time, a new message had arrived in the Chaotic News Network inbox. It read "hey cutie." It was in Comic Sans. It has been blocked.

It is still blinking.
