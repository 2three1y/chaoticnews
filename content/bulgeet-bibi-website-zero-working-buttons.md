A months-long investigation by Chaotic News Network has confirmed what visitors have long suspected: the official website of Bulgeet & Bibi Enterprises, LLC contains 41 buttons, and not one of them does anything.

Our team pressed all of them. Some twice. One intern pressed "DOWNLOAD ANYDESK" eleven times before being gently walked out of the building and given a juice box.

"Every single button opens a popup," said lead inspector Delphine Clickworth, holding a clipboard that had also stopped working. "The popup says 'Sir, your firewall is expired.' You press OK and a new popup says 'Congratulations sir, nothing happened.' I have never felt so congratulated for so little. Not even at my own graduation."

[Visit the Bulgeet & Bibi Enterprises website](https://2three1y.github.io/bulgeetandbibi/) at your own risk. Your firewall may already be expired.

## "Totally Legit Since Yesterday"

The company describes itself as "Registered in a basement" and lists its headquarters as a group chat called "$$$ BOSSES $$$," which has two members and one muted notification setting. Its website offers three products: Virus Removal, Firewall Installation and a monthly plan called Girlfriend-as-a-Service.

The Virus Removal service is a three-step process, according to the site: "We remove viruses. Then we add viruses. Then we remove those." Industry experts call this "a subscription."

The Firewall Installation package consists of an actual wall. The wall is on fire.

"Wall is on fire, fire is on wall," CEO Bulgeet said in a phone interview, which he opened by asking our reporter to buy a $500 gift card. "Very secure. Nobody is getting through that wall. Including the customer. Including me. I have not been home since March."

## A Complete Inventory of Buttons

CN inspectors catalogued every button on the site. A partial list:

- "BOOK NOW" plays a 6-second clip of a fax machine.
- "FREE QUOTE" opens a popup that says "Quote: 500 dollar."
- "MEET THE TEAM" shows one photo of 14 men in headsets, all labeled Steve.
- "UNSUBSCRIBE" subscribes you to three newsletters.
- "CANCEL" is not a button. It is a picture of a button. Inspectors pressed it for 47 minutes before noticing.

"The scrollbar is also decorative," Clickworth added. "You scroll by tilting the laptop."

## The Contact Form

The site's most ambitious feature is its Contact Us form, which asks for your name, a description of your virus, and "Gift card numbers (all of them)."

Pressing Submit does nothing. According to the site, the message "goes directly to the void, where Steve will read it."

"We tested this," Clickworth confirmed. "We wrote 'hello' and pressed Submit. Four minutes later, a man named Steve called to say our computer had a virus. He did not mention the form. He did not need to."

The form also includes a CAPTCHA that shows a single picture of a woman and asks visitors to "please identify this person." Every answer is marked correct. The correct answer is Bibi.

## Accessibility Praised, Somehow

In a surprise twist, accessibility testers gave the site high marks.

"Every button has a clear label," said Morris Tabstop, who tested the site with a screen reader. "So my screen reader could tell me exactly how useless each one was. I knew right away that 'BOOK NOW' would not book anything. Honestly, that's more respect than most websites give me."

The site's Accessibility Statement reads, in full: "Our website is 100% accessible to scammers." Below it is a single button labeled "Button." It does nothing. Tabstop gave it four stars.

> "We will make one button work by Tuesday. It will be the PAY NOW button."

That promise came from the company's Head of IT, who is also named Steve, and who said the team is "working on a fix" from a laptop that was, at the time, upside down.

Reached for comment, CFO Bibi sent our newsroom a message reading "hey cutie," followed by a selfie, followed by an invoice for the selfie.

As of press time, the website's visitor counter read 0000007. Sources confirm all seven visits were Bulgeet's mom, who left one review: "Very nice website. Could not find the button to call my son."
