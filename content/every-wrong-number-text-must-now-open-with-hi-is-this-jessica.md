A new industry standard now requires every “wrong number” scam text to begin with the words “Hi, is this Jessica?”, ending years of confusion among scammers who had been opening with “hey stranger” and “did you get my package.”

“Consistency builds trust,” said a spokesperson for the International Guild of Accidental Texters, who asked to be identified only as Steve. “If you get a text asking for Jessica, you will know exactly what kind of night it is going to be.”

Nobody has actually reached Jessica since 2019. Experts believe she changed her number, moved, or simply put her phone in a drawer and found peace.

Phone owners across the country say they are ready. “I just reply ‘no, this is her lawyer,’” said one area teen. “They leave pretty fast.”

The rules also say that if you answer “who is this?”, the scammer must reply with a photo of a sunset and the words “oops wrong number, but you seem nice 😊.” That part is unchanged.

Chaotic News reminds readers: if a stranger texts you looking for Jessica, you are not Jessica. Block and report. Jessica would want that.
