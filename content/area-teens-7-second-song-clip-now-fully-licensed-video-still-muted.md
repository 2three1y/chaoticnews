[The Verge](https://x.com/verge) posted on X that Universal and Warner music groups are working with Adobe on a library of fully licensed song clips. Somewhere, a teen who has had 400 videos muted felt a single tear roll down his cheek.

## What Actually Happened

Adobe is adding licensed music from Universal Music Group and Warner Music Group to **Premiere mobile**, its video editing app for phones. No specific artists have been named yet. Adobe only says "popular songs" from "leading artists," so you can jump on "the latest trend."

The feature is "coming soon." According to Adobe, you will be able to:

- add up to **60 seconds** of a popular track directly to a video
- browse, search and preview music by artist or track, filter by genre and length, and bookmark favorites
- use templates that already come paired with music
- export the finished video and post it to licensed social platforms

Premiere already has a library of royalty-free music, but right now licensed songs have to be imported by hand. The Verge says the update is meant to help Premiere mobile compete with apps like CapCut. The Verge also asked Adobe whether this is coming to the desktop Premiere Pro app, and was waiting to hear back.

## Our Take (This Part Is Made Up)

Yes, the real limit is 60 seconds, not seven. Our headline is wrong on purpose. Our local teen only needs seven seconds, because that is the part of the song where the beat drops.

He tells us his clip is now fully licensed, totally legal, and blessed by two record labels. The platform muted it anyway. "It's tradition at this point," he said. "Like Thanksgiving. But for copyright."
