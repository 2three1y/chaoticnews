A local teen reported a scammer to a messaging platform Saturday morning, and the scammer has responded by reporting that he feels "very attacked," "deeply disrespected" and "honestly kind of bullied by a child."

The scammer, who introduces himself as Steve from Microsoft but whose real name is Bulgeet, filed a three-page complaint with the platform's support team. Page one asks the support team for a $500 gift card. Pages two and three ask again, but sadder.

"I simply told him his computer had virus," Bulgeet wrote in the complaint, a copy of which was obtained by Chaotic News Network. "Instead of believing me, he took a screenshot. Then he took another screenshot. Then he pressed Report. Who raised him like this?"

The teen's mother confirmed that she raised him like this.

## The 94 Seconds That Changed Everything

According to the teen, who asked to be identified only as "the guy who pressed it," the entire conversation lasted 94 seconds.

"He said my firewall was expired," the teen said. "I said 'what firewall.' He said 'exactly.' Then he told me to download AnyDesk so he could 'go inside the computer.' I was not letting a man named Steve inside the computer. My mom doesn't even let me inside the computer after 10."

The teen described the reporting process as "honestly kind of relaxing, like popping bubble wrap."

"You press Report, you pick 'Scam or fraud,' you press Submit," he said. "Three buttons. All three worked. That's three more working buttons than his entire website."

## The Complaint

Bulgeet's complaint lists 11 grievances. Chief among them is that the teen "did not even let me finish."

"I had prepared a whole speech about the firewall," he wrote. "It had a beginning, a middle and a part where you buy gift cards. He skipped straight to the end. The end is not supposed to be Report. The end is supposed to be gift cards."

Other grievances include:

1. The teen replied "lol" four times, which Bulgeet called "psychological warfare in text form."
2. The teen asked "is your name really Steve," which Bulgeet called "an invasion of my Steve."
3. The teen's little brother, age 9, got on the call and asked if Bulgeet had a dog.

"I do not have a dog," Bulgeet wrote. "Thank you for asking. Nobody ever asks."

In a section titled "Damages," Bulgeet claims the report caused him "emotional distress, loss of income, and a very long lunch break," and requests compensation in the form of "one (1) Google Play card, any amount, I am not picky right now."

> "I have been Steve from Microsoft for six years. In that time, nobody has ever been this mean to me. Except all the other people who also pressed Report."

## Support Team Responds

The platform's support team reviewed the complaint and closed it in 0.4 seconds with the note "Working as intended."

"We get these every day," said support supervisor Janet Ticketson. "A scammer gets reported and feels like the victim. It's like a burglar leaving a one-star review because the house had locks. We have a template reply for it. It just says 'Lol' and closes the ticket."

Ticketson said Bulgeet then opened a second ticket titled "Appeal," then a third titled "Appeal of Appeal," and a fourth titled "Hey cutie," which was later traced to his business partner, Bibi.

## Experts Applaud

Online safety experts are calling the teen's response a perfect 94 seconds.

"No arguing, no downloading anything, no gift cards," said Dr. Felix Safemode of the Institute for Just Pressing Report. "Screenshot, Report, Block, done. That's the whole recipe. The scammer feeling attacked is just a little bonus, like sprinkles."

Dr. Safemode noted that the teen did make one mistake: "He said 'lol' only four times. In my clinical opinion, five is the healing number."

As of press time, Bulgeet had created a new account to message the teen and explain "his side of the story." It was reported in under four seconds.

Bulgeet says he feels "attacked again," and also "a little impressed."
