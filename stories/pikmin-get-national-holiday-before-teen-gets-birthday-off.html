Pikmin Get Their Own National Holiday Before Area Teen Gets a Day Off for His Birthday

The tiny plant creatures now have a whole day. The teen has a math test. “Honestly, the little guys are cooking,” a vice principal admitted.

SOMEWHERE ONLINE (CN) — Tiny plant creatures known as Pikmin now officially have their own holiday, officials confirmed Monday, while an area teen whose birthday falls next Thursday has been told that he still has a math test that day, and also gym.

“They’re like an inch tall,” said Ethan Vasquez-Moore, who turns 15 next week. “They don’t go to school. They don’t pay taxes. They have a leaf on their head. What did they do to earn a holiday?”

The Case for Pikmin

Plenty, according to the records. Data obtained by Chaotic News Network shows that Pikmin carried approximately 40,000 pieces of fruit across a cartoon garden this year, many of them while being thrown overhand by a small astronaut who did not ask their permission.

They also fought giant spiders, rebuilt a spaceship, and lifted a single strawberry the size of a car using a team of 25, with no complaints and a cheerful little song.

Over the same period, Ethan carried one backpack to school, complained about it daily and once described taking out the recycling as “basically a side quest I didn’t accept.”

“Honestly, the little guys are cooking,” admitted a vice principal at Ethan’s school, who asked not to be named because she “doesn’t want the kids to know she games.” “Have you seen them work? They never miss a day. They never say ‘it’s not my turn.’ We should all be more like the Pikmin.”

“Honestly, the little guys are cooking.”

The Case for Ethan

Ethan has filed a formal written request with the school board for an “Area Teen Day,” to be held every year on his birthday. The proposal, which runs four pages and uses the word “deserve” eleven times, outlines how the day would be celebrated:

  • no homework, worldwide
  • one large pepperoni pizza, delivered to him specifically
  • a moment of silence for his dead phone battery
  • his little sister must call him “sir”

The board has agreed to “look into it,” which experts say means no.

“When a school board says it will look into something, it is the same as a mom saying ‘we’ll see,’” said Dr. Fern Sproutley of the Institute for Holiday Fairness. “It has never once meant yes in recorded history. I checked. I went back to the 1800s.”

Dr. Sproutley says there are currently more holidays honoring small creatures than honoring teenagers. “Groundhogs have a day. Pikmin have a day. Teens have Monday.”

The Family Reacts

Ethan’s mother, Rosa, says she is trying to be supportive.

“I told him he could have a Pikmin-themed birthday,” she said. “He said that was ‘rubbing it in.’ I said fine, and then I bought the Pikmin cake anyway because it was on sale.”

His younger sister, Lucia, 8, has reportedly sided with the Pikmin.

“They’re cute and they listen,” she said. “Ethan is not cute and he doesn’t listen. He’s like a Pikmin that only carries snacks to his own room.”

Even Steve from Microsoft has weighed in. He called the family Sunday night to warn that Ethan’s birthday was “expired” and could be renewed for a $200 gift card. Ethan hung up. His mother says it was “the most responsible thing he did all week.”

The Countdown

With the Pikmin holiday officially on the calendar, toy stores are already selling out of leaf hats, and one local park has scheduled a fruit-carrying relay in their honor.

Ethan says he will attend the math test “in protest.”

At press time, Ethan had painted himself red, put a leaf on his head and walked silently into the principal’s office carrying an entire watermelon, where he was given a hall pass, a round of applause, and the rest of the day off.
