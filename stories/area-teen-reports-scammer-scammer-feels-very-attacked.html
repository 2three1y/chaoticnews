Area Teen Reports Scammer, Scammer Reports Feeling “Very Attacked”

After being reported, blocked and screenshotted, a scammer filed a formal complaint about the “toxic environment” of people who refuse to be scammed.

By Lorraine Static, Breaking News Desk · Saturday, October 31, 2026, 10:20 AM

Chaotic News is satire. All stories are made up.

An area teen reported a scammer on Saturday morning, and the scammer has responded by reporting that he feels “very attacked,” “deeply disrespected” and “honestly kind of bullied.”

The scammer, who calls himself Steve from Microsoft but whose real name is Bulgeet, filed a three-page complaint with the platform’s support team. He opened it by asking the support team for a $500 gift card.

“I simply told him his computer had virus,” Bulgeet wrote in the complaint, a copy of which was obtained by Chaotic News Network. “Instead of believing me, he took a screenshot. Then he took another screenshot. Then he pressed Report. Who raised him like this?”

What Happened

According to the teen, the conversation lasted less than two minutes.

“He said my firewall was expired,” the teen told our reporter, asking that we not use their name. “I said ‘what firewall.’ He said ‘exactly.’ Then he asked me to download AnyDesk, and I just pressed Report. It was right there. It’s a really good button.”

The teen described the reporting process as “honestly kind of relaxing.”

“You press Report, you pick ‘Scam or fraud,’ you press Submit,” the teen said. “Unlike the buttons on his website, it actually works.”

The Complaint

Bulgeet’s complaint lists a number of grievances. Chief among them is that the teen “did not even let me finish.”

“I had prepared a whole speech about the firewall,” he wrote. “It had a beginning, a middle and a part where you buy gift cards. He skipped straight to the end. The end is not supposed to be Report. The end is supposed to be iTunes.”

The complaint also alleges that the teen “laughed in text form,” using “lol” four times, which Bulgeet described as “psychological warfare.”

In a second section, titled “Damages,” Bulgeet claims that being reported has caused him “emotional distress, loss of income, and a very long lunch break.”

“I am a professional,” the complaint concludes. “I have been Steve from Microsoft for six years. In that time, nobody has ever been this mean to me. Except all the other people who also pressed Report.”

Support Team Responds

The platform’s support team reviewed the complaint and closed it within seconds, with the note “Working as intended.”

A spokesperson for the support team, Janet Ticketson, said the response was “standard procedure.”

“We get complaints like this every day,” Ticketson said. “A scammer gets reported and feels like the victim. It’s like a burglar complaining that the house had locks. We have a template reply. It just says ‘Lol’ and closes the ticket.”

Experts Applaud

Online safety experts are praising the teen’s response as a model for everyone.

“No arguing, no downloading anything, no gift cards,” said Dr. Felix Safemode, who studies scams for a living. “Screenshot, Report, Block, done. That’s the whole recipe. The scammer feeling attacked is just a little bonus.”

As of press time, Bulgeet had created a new account to message the teen and explain “his side of the story.” It was reported in under four seconds.

He says he feels “attacked again.”
