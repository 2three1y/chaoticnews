Area Teen Spends 6 Hours Power Washing Barbie’s DreamHouse, Has Not Seen His Own Bedroom Floor Since August

The DreamHouse is now 100% clean. His room is 0% clean. “It’s different,” he explained. “There’s a percentage bar.”

By Trent Respawn, Gaming Editor

SOMEWHERE ONLINE (CN) — An area teen spent six straight hours Monday carefully power washing every inch of a virtual pink DreamHouse, including the hot tub, the three-story slide and the underside of a toy convertible, while his actual bedroom floor remains buried under a layer of hoodies first reported in August.

“The hot tub had grime on it,” said Owen Fairbrook, 15. “I couldn’t just leave it there. Barbie has guests.”

Owen did not stop for dinner. He did not stop for homework. He paused once, at the 4-hour mark, to ask his mother for a juice box, which she slid under the door.

The Room

According to Owen’s mother, Karen Fairbrook, the bedroom floor has not been visible since roughly August 9, the last day she personally saw a patch of it near the closet.

“I think it’s carpet,” she said. “It was carpet. I believe it is still carpet. At this point I can’t rule out hardwood.”

A Chaotic News Network reporter was allowed into the room for exactly 90 seconds, wearing hiking boots. Items catalogued during the visit included:

  • 23 hoodies, all gray, one possibly a cat
  • a cereal bowl that has developed its own small weather system
  • a sock last seen at the beginning of the school year, now emotionally different
  • three chargers, none of which charge anything in the house
  • the TV remote the family declared missing in September, which Owen called “a coincidence”

“I’ve covered a lot of stories,” said our reporter. “I have never had to step over a hoodie and have the hoodie step back.”

“When I clean my room, nothing tells me I’m at 87 percent. Where’s the little chime?”

“There’s a Percentage Bar”

Owen insists the two situations are completely different.

“In the game there’s a percentage bar,” he explained. “When you finish a wall, it goes ding. It sparkles. When I clean my room, nothing tells me I’m at 87 percent. Where’s the satisfying sound? Where’s the little chime?”

Researchers have confirmed that the bedroom does not have a chime.

“This is extremely common,” said Dr. Sudsy Pressman, director of the Institute for Virtual Chores. “Teens will happily clean a fake mansion for six hours. But a real room gives no feedback. Nobody claps when you pick up a sock. The sock does not sparkle. It is just a sock, and now you are holding it.”

Dr. Pressman’s research shows that the average teen will power wash 4,000 square feet of digital patio before voluntarily touching one real dish.

Mom Strikes Back

Mrs. Fairbrook says she has tried everything. She offered money. She offered pizza. She offered to let him use the good Wi-Fi spot in the hallway.

Then she got creative. She printed a fake percentage bar and taped it to the bedroom door. It reads “ROOM: 0% CLEAN.” She has promised to update it by hand with a marker every time an item leaves the floor.

“I also learned to make the ding,” she said. “I just say it. ‘Ding.’ Out loud. He hates it. But yesterday he picked up a hoodie, and I said ‘Ding,’ and I swear I saw something in his eyes.”

His younger brother, Caleb, 11, has offered to clean the room himself for $40, “or $60 if I have to touch the bowl.”

The DreamHouse Today

The finished DreamHouse is reportedly spotless, gleaming and so clean it has started reflecting the sun in the game’s sky. Owen says Barbie has not personally thanked him.

“She doesn’t have to,” he said. “I know what I did.”

At press time, his mother had placed a power washer sticker on the vacuum cleaner. Owen approached it, stared at it for several minutes, then went back to the game because he had missed a spot on the flamingo.
