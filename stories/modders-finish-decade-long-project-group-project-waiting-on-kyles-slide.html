Modders Finish Decade-Long Project While Area Teen’s Group Project, Also Started in 2016, Still Waiting on Kyle’s Slide

A volunteer team built a complete set of game tools in their spare time. Kyle has not opened the slideshow, but he did react with a thumbs up in 2019.

SOMEWHERE ONLINE (CN) — A team of volunteer modders this week finished a project ten years in the making, releasing a complete set of tools for a classic space horror game. Meanwhile, a group project on the water cycle, assigned to four students the very same year, is still waiting on one slide from Kyle.

“We all did our parts in like a day,” said group member Priya Vandermolen, who was in sixth grade when the project began and is now a sophomore in college. “Slide 4 just says ‘KYLE PUT YOUR STUFF HERE.’ In red. Size 72 font. It has said that for a decade.”

Ten Years, Two Teams

The modding team, which goes by the name Hull Breach Collective, says its eleven volunteers worked nights and weekends, kept careful notes, answered every message and held 412 video calls. Their finished release contains more than 90,000 lines of code.

Kyle’s slide contains zero lines of anything.

“The modders had a roadmap,” said Vandermolen. “We had a roadmap too. It was one step long. Step one was Kyle.”

Over the past ten years, the group chat, titled “WATER CYCLE SQUAD,” has received 3,118 messages. Kyle has read all of them. The read receipts prove it. He has replied to none.

“He did react with a thumbs up once,” said group member Jordan Okonkwo-Bellweather. “In 2019. We threw a party. We thought it meant he was starting. It turned out he had meant to thumbs up a photo of a sandwich in a different chat.”

“Slide 4 just says ‘KYLE PUT YOUR STUFF HERE.’ It has said that for a decade.”

A Brief History of Kyle’s Slide

According to a timeline assembled by Chaotic News Network from the group’s shared slideshow history:

  1. 2016: Slide 4 created. Kyle assigned “Evaporation.”
  2. 2017: Someone adds a clip-art cloud to Slide 4 “to get him started.”
  3. 2018: Kyle is spotted viewing the slideshow for 0.4 seconds.
  4. 2020: The cloud is updated to a sadder cloud.
  5. 2022: The slideshow app goes through four redesigns. Kyle’s slide survives all of them, empty, like a monument.
  6. 2026: Water continues to evaporate without Kyle’s help.

The teacher who assigned the project, Mrs. Delores Fenwick, has since retired, moved to Florida and taken up competitive pottery. She says she still checks her school email “every Easter, just in case.”

“I extended the due date 46 times,” Fenwick said, while trimming a vase. “The last extension was to ‘whenever Kyle is ready.’ I think about Slide 4 more than I think about my own grandchildren. Please don’t print that.” We printed it.

The Science of Kyle

Experts say every group project in history has contained at least one Kyle.

“We’ve studied this for years,” said Dr. Ingrid Slacksworth, director of the Center for Group Project Studies. “Even when there are only three people in a group, a fourth person will appear and his name will be Kyle. If you remove Kyle, one of the remaining students will slowly become Kyle. It is like a law of physics. Like gravity, but for doing nothing.”

Dr. Slacksworth estimates that if the world’s Kyles finished their slides all at once, global productivity would rise by 11 percent and every teacher in America would retire on the same day.

The modders, for their part, say they are sympathetic. “We’ve offered to help,” said Hull Breach Collective lead Tobias Rennard. “We reverse-engineered a game from 2008. We can definitely do one slide about evaporation. But the group says it has to be Kyle. It’s become about the principle.”

Kyle Speaks

Reached for comment Monday at his home, where he was playing a different game for the sixth straight hour, Kyle Brackett, now 22, appeared surprised.

“Oh, that’s due?” he said.

He then asked our reporter if the slide could be “like, just a picture.”

At press time, Kyle had opened the slideshow for the first time in seven years, changed the font on Slide 4 to Comic Sans, typed “evaporation is when,” and logged off, saying he would “finish it after this round.”
