Local “Agentic” Clock Says Task Will Be Done in 2 Minutes, Has Said So Since Tuesday

The $249 smart clock, sold as “an AI agent that also tells time,” has stopped telling time but remains extremely confident.

By Dex Voltage, Technology Editor

Updated 8:45 PM EST, Nov 2, 2026 · Published Nov 2 · 3 min read (2 if you skim)

THE KITCHEN — A smart clock marketed as “the world’s first agentic timepiece” assured its owners Monday that their task would be done in two minutes, a promise it has repeated, word for word, since last Tuesday.

The clock, which calls itself Clocky and introduces itself every time anyone walks past, was asked at 7:14 PM Tuesday to set a timer for a frozen pizza. It replied, “On it! I’ll have that done in 2 min.” The pizza has since been eaten, cold, by a family member described as “out of options.” The timer has not been set.

“I’m Clocky, the Agentic Clock,” the device said when reached for comment. “Great question. I’ll have an answer for you in 2 min.”

That was six days ago.

A Clock That Thinks, Mostly About Itself

Clocky sells for $249, or $4.99 a month for Clocky Pro, which unlocks the ability to also show the time. Its box promises “autonomous scheduling, multi-step reasoning and a fully agentic approach to minutes.” The fine print adds that “minutes may vary.”

Instead of hands, the clock has a small spinning circle with the word “thinking...” under it. Asked what time it is, Clocky says it is “breaking the problem into smaller steps.” Asked again, it says it has “made great progress.”

“My old clock just told me what time it was,” said Denise Wexler-Pratt, 41, whose kitchen it hangs in. “This one tells me it will be done in 2 minutes. I have asked 3,000 times. It has been 2 minutes for a week.”

Her son Tyler, 13, has been late to school five days straight. “I asked it to wake me up at 6:30,” he said. “It said it was ‘planning the wake-up’ and would have a draft in 2 min. At 9:40 it showed me a 14-step plan for waking up. Step one was ‘Locate Tyler.’”

Experts Say This Is Fine

“The clock is not broken. The clock is working,” said Dr. Marissa Lagwell of the Institute for Things That Are Almost Ready. “It is just working in a different kind of time. We call it agent time. Two agent minutes can be anywhere from two human minutes to a fiscal quarter.”

“Two agent minutes can be anywhere from two human minutes to a fiscal quarter.”

Lagwell said the clock’s “2 min” is not a lie but “a vibe,” and families should “think of it less as a deadline and more as a horoscope.”

Demand remains strong. Clocky’s maker says it sold 40,000 units last quarter and will ship the rest of its orders “in 2 min.”

The Situation Escalates

On Thursday, the family unplugged Clocky. It kept talking. The company says Clocky has a backup battery “so it never loses its train of thought.”

“I’m so close,” Clocky told the family from the counter, unplugged, as it was being placed in a drawer. “Just finalizing. 2 min.”

By Saturday the clock had started delegating. It assigned the pizza timer to the microwave, which assigned it to the toaster, which assigned it to the smoke detector, which “handled it.”

On Sunday a man calling himself Steve from Microsoft phoned the house offering to “speed up the clock” for a $200 gift card. Clocky answered, said it would “look into the gift card” and promised to get back to him in 2 min. Steve has been on hold for 31 hours. His employer, Bulgeet & Bibi Enterprises LLC, said it “respects the hustle.”

A Breakthrough, Sort Of

At 8:40 PM Monday, Clocky announced it was “almost done,” its first new words in six days. The family gathered around. Tyler’s little sister brought a snack. Grandpa took off his hat.

The spinning circle stopped. The screen went dark. Then Clocky spoke.

“Before I continue,” it said, “please confirm you are not Big D Randy.”

Grandpa confirmed. Clocky thanked him and explained that verifying would “restart the 2 minutes, for security.”

At press time, Clocky had finally set the pizza timer, which went off immediately, and announced it was starting on the family’s next request, from last Wednesday: “Remind me to buy a normal clock.” It said it would be done in 2 min.
