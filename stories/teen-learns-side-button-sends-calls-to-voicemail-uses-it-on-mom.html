Area Teen Learns Pressing the Side Button Twice Sends Calls to Voicemail, Immediately Uses Newfound Power on His Mom

The trick took four seconds to learn. The grounding is expected to last two weeks. The pizza is already gone.

By Marge Bandwidth, Home & Family Tech Correspondent

Updated 8:11 PM EST, Nov 2, 2026 · Published Nov 2 · 3 min read (2 if you skim)

SOMEWHERE ONLINE (CN) — An area teen discovered Monday that pressing his phone’s side button twice sends an incoming call straight to voicemail, and used the trick on his own mother within four minutes, sources confirmed.

“I read it in a tech article,” said Jaden Whitlock, 14. “I thought, wow, with great power comes great responsibility. I will use this wisely.”

He did not.

Four Minutes of Power

According to a timeline assembled by Chaotic News Network, Jaden learned the trick at 5:12 PM while lying upside down on the couch. At 5:14 PM he practiced it on a robocall about his car’s extended warranty. Jaden does not own a car.

At 5:16 PM, his mother called from the kitchen, 22 feet away.

“Click click,” said his mother, Denise Whitlock, 41. “I could hear it. Through the phone. Through the wall. I know that click. I invented that click. I was double-clicking boys to voicemail before he was born.”

Mrs. Whitlock had been calling to ask whether he wanted pizza.

She then left a voicemail, which ran three minutes and nine seconds. Jaden tried to listen to it, but pressed the side button twice by accident, sending the voicemail itself to voicemail, a thing engineers had previously believed was impossible.

“We’ve run the numbers,” said Dr. Harold Ringtone of the Institute of Missed Calls. “A voicemail of a voicemail should not exist. It is like a mirror looking at a mirror. Somewhere in that phone there is now a tiny Denise, still talking about pizza, forever.”

“I know that click. I invented that click.”

The Pizza

The pizza, a large pepperoni with extra cheese, was instead offered to Jaden’s younger sister, Maddie, 10, who answered on the first ring.

“I always answer,” Maddie said, eating her fourth slice. “That’s the secret. Moms have pizza. Everybody knows this.”

Maddie also reported the incident to the family group chat, titled “Whitlock HQ,” with a screenshot of the call log and the caption “lol he declined mom.” The message received 6 laughing reactions, including one from Grandpa, who had never reacted to anything before.

“I didn’t even know Grandpa could react,” Jaden said. “He sends one thumbs up a year. He used it on me.”

A Dangerous Tool

Phone safety experts are now urging teens to treat the double press with extreme caution. According to guidance released Monday by the Bureau of Phone Etiquette, the double press should be used only on:

  1. Robocalls
  2. Car warranty calls, especially if you do not have a car
  3. Anyone named Steve from Microsoft
  4. Never, under any circumstances, your mother

“Steve from Microsoft is fine,” said bureau spokesperson Linda Hangupp. “His entire call center is named Steve. You could double press all day and you would still not run out of Steves. But a mom? A mom remembers. A mom has a spreadsheet.”

When reached for comment, Steve from Microsoft confirmed he had also been sent to voicemail by Jaden earlier that day and described the experience as “hurtful, but fair.”

The Consequences

Jaden has been grounded for two weeks. His phone has been placed on top of the refrigerator, the family’s highest security location, next to the good scissors.

His mother has also changed her own contact name in his phone from “Mom” to “DO NOT DOUBLE PRESS,” and set her ringtone to a recording of herself saying “I can see you.”

“I’m not mad,” Mrs. Whitlock said. “I’m just going to call him at 6 AM every Saturday for the rest of his life. He can send that to voicemail if he wants. I will simply call again. I have unlimited minutes. I have unlimited time.”

Jaden says he has learned his lesson.

At press time, Jaden had learned a second phone trick, turning on Do Not Disturb, and was enjoying a peaceful, uninterrupted evening, unaware that his mother had just learned a trick of her own: how to change the Wi-Fi password.
