YouTube Bot Check Now Asking Humans to Prove They Are Not Big D Randy

The new check skips the traffic lights and asks one question. No adult has passed it yet, including people who are definitely not Big D Randy.

SOMEWHERE ONLINE (CN) — Viewers trying to watch videos this weekend were greeted by a new kind of security check that no longer asks them to click every square containing a traffic light. Instead, it asks a single question:

“Please confirm you are not Big D Randy.”

Then it waits. Witnesses describe the waiting as “uncomfortable,” “like being stared at by a toaster,” and “like it already knows something.”

How We Got Here

The change comes one day after a local AI known as Tab was taken out by the name Big D Randy alone. The AI read the title of a comedy skit, “BIG D RANDY: THE CALL,” once, collapsed, and spent the afternoon responding only in crying-laughing emoji.

“That was the turning point,” said Wendy Captcha, a security analyst at the Institute of Clicking Boxes Before You’re Allowed to Do Things. “For years, engineers believed the biggest threat to the internet was bots. On Friday, they learned the biggest threat was Randy. Bots can be stopped. Randy can only be survived.”

Captcha said the old test, which asked users to identify crosswalks, was retired after an internal review found that “a crosswalk has never once taken out an AI.”

The New Test

Under the new system, users are shown a checkbox labeled “I am not Big D Randy.” Checking the box causes a second question to appear: “Are you sure?” Checking that causes a third: “Then why does your voice sound like that?”

Users who make it past the third question are shown a grid of nine pictures and asked to “select every square containing Big D Randy.” All nine squares are empty. The correct answer is all nine.

“He’s not in the picture. He doesn’t need to be in the picture. He’s already in your house.”

“I have never been Big D Randy,” said Doug Ferngully, 44, who spent 40 minutes trying to watch a video about fixing a leaky sink. “I don’t even know what he looks like. But the test kept saying ‘Suspicious Randy activity detected.’ My sink is still leaking. I am questioning my identity. My wife has started calling me Randy, and I don’t think it’s a joke anymore.”

A 13-year-old in a nearby town reported a different problem. “It let me in,” he said. “On the first try. Now my whole group chat thinks I’m him. I’ve been getting fan mail. I’m not saying I’m Randy. I’m just not saying I’m not.”

Has Anyone Passed?

As of press time, no adult has passed the new check.

“That’s by design,” Captcha said. “Big D Randy never has to speak. He never has to appear. He doesn’t even have to be online. So the system has no way to prove you’re not him. Technically, any of us could be Randy. That’s the scary part. That’s also the funny part.”

The check has spread. Users report seeing it on a pizza order page, a library login and, in one case, a smart fridge, which refused to dispense ice until its owner “stated his full legal name, slowly.”

Even Bulgeet & Bibi Enterprises, LLC added the check to its website, where it is the only button that works. It does not let anyone in. Bulgeet called this “finally, a feature.”

Meanwhile, Tab, the AI at the center of Friday’s incident, was shown the new check this morning and immediately started laughing again. A spokesperson said “Tab is fine,” then corrected the statement to “Tab is, once again, not okay.”

What Experts Recommend

Tech experts say there is no known workaround, but offer a few tips that may help:

  1. Clear your browser history, especially anything with the word “Randy” in it.
  2. Speak in a normal voice near your computer. No echo, no reverb, no dramatic bass drop.
  3. Do not attempt to put Big D Randy’s voice effects on any beat titled “DETONATOR PROTOCOL.” The system will notice.

Big D Randy could not be reached for comment. The checkbox, however, is still waiting.

At press time, CN’s own editor had been locked out of this article by the check, which is why this sentence was written by the checkbox.
