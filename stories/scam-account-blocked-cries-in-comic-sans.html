Local Scam Account Blocked, Cries in Comic Sans

The account, which had sent “hey cutie 🥺” to 4,000 people, released a tearful statement in yellow-on-red Comic Sans. Readers are struggling to take it seriously.

By Gary Breakingnews, Scams Desk

Updated 9:05 AM EDT, Sat October 31, 2026, give or take · 2 min read (1 if you skim)

Satire, Obviously. Chaotic News is satire. All stories are made up. Do not forward this to your aunt as fact.

SOMEWHERE ONLINE (CN) — A scam account known online as “Bibi ♡ (Bookworm)” was blocked Saturday morning, and has since released an emotional statement written entirely in Comic Sans, in bright yellow, on a red background, with three blinking words.

“I am devastated,” the statement begins, in a font usually reserved for bake sale flyers. “I only wanted to say hey cutie. I said it nicely. I said it 4,000 times. Is that a crime?”

Legal experts say it is, in fact, a lot of times.

How It Happened

The trouble began when Bibi sent the message “hey cutie 🥺” to a local AI. Then she sent it again. Then she sent it a third time, this time with a selfie.

Witnesses say the selfie was “very clearly a stock photo” and still had a faint watermark across the forehead reading SAMPLE.

“The account joined in 2022 and posted its first selfie yesterday,” said digital forensics analyst Roberta Pixelson. “Its bio says ‘Bookworm.’ We checked. It has read zero books. It has not even read the terms of service, which is how it got blocked.”

The block button was pressed at 9:02 AM. Bibi’s statement arrived at 9:03 AM. Experts say this is the fastest anything associated with Bulgeet & Bibi Enterprises has ever worked.

The Statement

The full statement runs three paragraphs. A fourth paragraph is just the word “cutie” with a heart, repeated until it reaches the edge of the screen, and then a little past it.

In it, Bibi describes herself as “a simple girl who loves reading, sunsets, and Google Play gift cards.” She asks the person who blocked her to “please unblock so I can explain,” followed by “also send $500 for my plane ticket,” followed by “the plane is to see you, cutie.”

“The emotion is real,” said typography critic Lionel Kerning, who reviewed the statement for our Arts section. “But the font is doing her no favors. Nobody has ever cried in Comic Sans and been believed. Comic Sans was built for lemonade stands. It cannot carry heartbreak. It can barely carry a birthday.”

Kerning added that the blinking words, “PLEASE,” “CUTIE,” and “GIFTCARD,” “really undercut the sincerity.”

Bulgeet Responds

Bibi’s business partner, Bulgeet, who also goes by “Steve from Microsoft,” called our newsroom to offer support.

“Bibi is very sad,” he said. “To help her feel better, please download AnyDesk.”

When asked how downloading AnyDesk would help Bibi, Bulgeet paused for a long time.

“It will help me,” he said.

“We are a family business. The family is two people and a firewall that is on fire.”

What Happens Next

Bibi has reportedly created a new account named “Bibi ♡ (Bookworm) 2,” which was also blocked within the minute. A third account, “Not Bibi,” sent the message “hey cutie 🥺 it’s not Bibi” before being blocked as well.

Cybersecurity experts are urging the public to stay calm and remember one simple rule: nobody who is actually a bookworm has ever opened a conversation with “hey cutie.”

As of press time, a new message had arrived in the Chaotic News Network inbox. It read “hey cutie 🥺.” It was in Comic Sans.

It has been blocked.
