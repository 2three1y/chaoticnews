Memory Prices Hit “Unprecedented” Levels as Area Teen’s 16GB Laptop Is Formally Appraised as a Family Heirloom

Grandma has already asked who gets it in the will. The laptop has a frog sticker, a cracked corner and a Y key that only works if you mean it.

SOMEWHERE ONLINE (CN) — With computer memory prices reaching what analysts call “unprecedented” and “honestly kind of rude” levels, an area teen’s 16GB laptop was formally appraised Sunday as a family heirloom, making it the most valuable object in a house that also contains a car.

“It’s got a crack in the corner and a frog sticker on it,” said Mason Teague, 15, who bought the laptop used in 2023 for $240 and a Pokémon card. “Apparently that makes it vintage now.”

The appraiser, a man named Reginald Bytesworth who arrived in white gloves and a bow tie, spent 40 minutes examining the machine with a magnifying glass before declaring it worth “more than the car, less than the house, and about the same as the dog.”

The dog, a beagle named Waffles, has not been told.

Under Glass

The laptop now sits in a glass display case in the family living room, beside a framed photo of Great-Grandpa and a lamp that nobody is allowed to turn on. A small brass plaque reads: “16 GIGABYTES. PLEASE DO NOT TOUCH. THIS MEANS YOU, BRAYDEN.”

Brayden, 9, is Mason’s younger brother. He has been banned from the living room “until further notice” after he was caught opening 47 Chrome tabs “to see what would happen.”

“The fan made a noise like an airplane,” Brayden said. “Mom screamed. It was the best day of my life.”

Under the new family rules, Mason may use the laptop for 30 minutes a day, supervised, seated, with both feet on the floor. Snacks must stay at least six feet away. Liquids must stay in a different room.

“I used to play games on it,” Mason said. “Now I have to fill out a form. There’s a sign-in sheet. My mom stands behind me like a museum guard and says ‘gently’ every time I press a key.”

“I want the RAM. The rest you can split.”

The Will

The family’s estate planning has already begun. Mason’s grandmother, Doris Teague, 78, phoned the family lawyer Monday morning to formally request the laptop’s memory sticks.

“I want the RAM,” she said. “The rest you can split.”

When asked what she would do with it, Doris said she does not own a computer and “doesn’t plan to start.” She simply wants it “for the grandchildren’s grandchildren.”.

Mason’s uncle Phil has reportedly asked for the charger. His cousin Kayleigh has asked for the frog sticker. Waffles was not consulted.

A Market Gone Wild

Experts say the RAM market has never looked like this.

“A year ago, 16 gigabytes was normal,” said Dr. Celeste Megabyte, chief analyst at the Bureau of Things That Used to Be Cheap. “Today, 16 gigabytes is a down payment. By spring, it will be a personality.”

According to Dr. Megabyte, the price of a single 8GB stick has passed several notable benchmarks this month, including:

  • a used jet ski
  • one year of college textbooks
  • the entire contents of a vending machine, including the machine

Families across the country are now searching attics, garages and the backs of closets for old computers. One man in Ohio found his father’s 2009 desktop under a pile of Christmas lights and has since traded it for a boat.

“The boat was 32 feet,” Dr. Megabyte confirmed. “The desktop had 4 gigs. That’s the market now.”

Scammers have noticed too. Steve from Microsoft has reportedly updated his script and now calls customers to warn them “your RAM is expired” and must be mailed to a basement registered to Bulgeet & Bibi Enterprises, LLC, “for safe keeping.”

Looking Ahead

Mason says the attention has been strange. A girl in his science class asked if she could “just hold it for a second.” His teacher has started calling him “the kid with the RAM.”

“I just wanted to play Minecraft,” he said. “Now I have security.”

At press time, Mason’s mother had added the laptop to the family’s home insurance policy, which now costs more per month than the laptop itself, and Brayden had been spotted near the glass case holding a juice box.
