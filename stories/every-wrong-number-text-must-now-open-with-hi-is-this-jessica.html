Every Wrong-Number Text Is Now Legally Required to Open With “Hi, Is This Jessica?”

Jessica has not been reached since 2019. The Guild of Accidental Texters says it will keep trying, mostly at night, mostly with sunsets.

By Gary Breakingnews, Scams Desk

SOMEWHERE ONLINE (CN) — A new industry standard took effect Sunday requiring every “wrong number” scam text in the country to begin with the words “Hi, is this Jessica?”, ending years of confusion among scammers who had been opening with “hey stranger,” “did you get my package” and, in one documented case, “the eagle has landed.”

The rule was adopted by a 212-to-1 vote of the International Guild of Accidental Texters at its annual conference, held this year in a parking lot behind a phone repair kiosk. The one “no” vote came from a member who wanted it to be “Hi, is this Jennifer?” He has since been reassigned to Jessica.

“Consistency builds trust,” said Guild spokesperson Steve, who asked to be identified only as Steve. “When a stranger texts you asking for Jessica, you will know exactly what kind of night it is going to be. That’s our promise to the customer.”

The Search for Jessica

According to Guild records, nobody has actually reached Jessica since 2019.

In that time, an estimated 4.6 billion texts have been sent asking for her. Investigators believe she changed her number, moved, or simply put her phone in a kitchen drawer and found peace.

“We have texted Jessica at every phone number that exists,” said Guild records officer Marcy Fakelink. “Some numbers that don’t exist. One fax machine. She is very hard to find. Honestly, we respect her.”

Fakelink said the Guild briefly considered texting “Hi, is this Jessica’s mom?” to “approach from a different angle,” but the idea was dropped after 41,000 actual moms replied with three paragraphs and a recipe.

What the New Rules Require

The full standard runs 19 pages. CN has obtained a copy. Key requirements include:

  1. Every text must open with “Hi, is this Jessica?”
  2. If the recipient replies “who is this,” the sender must reply “oops wrong number, but you seem nice,” followed by a photo of a sunset.
  3. The sunset must not be a real sunset the sender saw. It must be from the internet.
  4. By the third message, the sender must mention that they “work in crypto” or “have a yacht.”
  5. Under no circumstances may the sender ever actually be looking for Jessica.

Rule 5 was added after a member found a real Jessica in 2022 and “had no idea what to say next.” He reportedly panicked, asked her how the weather was, and resigned.

Phone Owners Are Ready

Across the country, phone owners say the new standard has made their lives easier.

“I just reply ‘no, this is her lawyer,’” said one area teen. “They leave pretty fast. One time I said ‘no, this is her cat’ and he kept going for twenty minutes. That was on me.”

Another teen reported replying “Jessica is in the group chat now” and adding the scammer to a group chat with his entire swim team. The scammer left after 11 seconds and 340 notifications.

A local mom, Denise Overshare, said she has been receiving the texts for years and “always” responds.

“I tell them Jessica sounds lovely and they should call her more,” she said. “Then I tell them about my sciatica. They never text back. Nobody ever texts back.”

“She is very hard to find. Honestly, we respect her.”

Jessica Responds, Sort Of

Late Sunday, CN received a message from an unknown number reading “Hi, is this Chaotic News? This is Jessica.”

CN staff were briefly thrilled before the second message arrived, which asked for “a small fee to verify that I am Jessica,” payable in Google Play gift cards. The account was traced to Bulgeet & Bibi Enterprises, LLC, whose website was found to have a new button labeled “BE JESSICA.” It does not work.

Cybersecurity expert Dr. Lorna Blocklist of the Institute of Not Replying reminded the public that the safest response to any Jessica text is no response at all.

“You are not Jessica,” Dr. Blocklist said. “Block and report. Do not send a sunset back. Do not ask about the yacht. Jessica would want that.”

At press time, Dr. Blocklist’s phone buzzed. It was a text. It asked if she was Jessica.
