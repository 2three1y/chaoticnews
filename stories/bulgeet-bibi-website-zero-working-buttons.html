Bulgeet & Bibi Enterprises Website Still Has Zero Working Buttons

Inspectors pressed every button on the site. Every button pressed back with a popup. “Congratulations sir, nothing happened,” one of them said.

By Penny Popup, Consumer Affairs Correspondent · Saturday, October 31, 2026, 8:15 AM

Chaotic News is satire. All stories are made up.

A months-long investigation by Chaotic News Network has confirmed what visitors have long suspected: the official website of Bulgeet & Bibi Enterprises, LLC contains dozens of buttons, and not one of them does anything.

Our team pressed all of them. Some twice. One intern pressed “DOWNLOAD ANYDESK” eleven times before being gently removed from the building.

“Every single button opens a popup,” said lead inspector Delphine Clickworth, holding a clipboard that had also stopped working. “The popup says something like ‘Sir, your firewall is expired.’ Then you press another button and it says ‘Congratulations sir, nothing happened.’ I have never felt so congratulated for so little.”

Visit the Bulgeet & Bibi Enterprises website at your own risk. Your firewall may already be expired.

“Totally Legit Since Yesterday”

The company, which describes itself as “Registered in a basement” and headquartered in a group chat called “$$$ BOSSES $$$” (two members), launched its website to offer Virus Removal, Firewall Installation and a monthly product called Girlfriend-as-a-Service.

The Virus Removal service is described on the site as a three-step process: “We remove viruses. Then we add viruses. Then we remove those.” Industry experts call this “a subscription.”

The Firewall Installation package consists of an actual wall. The wall is on fire.

“Wall is on fire, fire is on wall,” CEO Bulgeet said in a phone interview, which he opened by asking our reporter to buy a $500 gift card. “Very secure. Nobody is getting through that wall. Including the customer.”

The Contact Form

Perhaps the most ambitious feature on the site is its Contact Us form, which asks for your name, a description of your virus, and “Gift card numbers (all of them).”

Pressing Submit does nothing. According to the site itself, the message “goes directly to the void, where Steve will read it.”

“We tested this,” Clickworth confirmed. “We wrote ‘hello’ and pressed Submit. Four minutes later, a man named Steve called to say our computer had a virus. He did not mention the form. He did not need to.”

The form also includes a CAPTCHA, which shows a single emoji of a woman and asks visitors to “please identify this person.” Every answer is marked correct. The correct answer is Bibi.

Accessibility Praised, Somehow

In a surprise twist, accessibility testers gave the site high marks.

“Every button has a clear label,” said Morris Tabstop, who tested the site with a screen reader. “So my screen reader could tell me exactly how useless each one was. That’s honestly more than most websites do. I knew immediately that ‘BOOK NOW’ would not book anything. I respect the honesty.”

The site’s own Accessibility Statement reads, in full: “Our website is 100% accessible to scammers.” Below it is a single button labeled “Button.” It does nothing.

Company Responds

Reached for comment, CFO Bibi ♡ sent our newsroom a message that read “hey cutie 🥺,” followed by a selfie, followed by an invoice.

Head of IT, also named Steve, said the team is “working on a fix.”

“We will make one button work by Tuesday,” he said. “It will be the PAY NOW button.”

As of press time, the website’s visitor counter read 0000007. Sources confirm all seven visits were Bulgeet’s mom.

“She is our biggest customer,” Bulgeet said. “She has not paid either.”
